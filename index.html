    Daytime feeding

    Wait for hunger, or feed at set times?

    Somewhere in between: keep a loose schedule, with feeds further apart.

    • Right now he has about 6 feeds a day of around 80ml each. That's snacking. Aim for 4–5 feeds, 3.5–4h apart, so he arrives at each one actually hungry.
    • Don't offer milk in between to fill gaps. Distract him with play, water from a cup with meals, or a walk.
    • If he refuses, stop after about 5 minutes. Try once more 20–30 minutes later, and if he still won't take it, wait until the next feed. Repeatedly pushing the bottle tends to make refusal worse.
    • Keep each feed to 20–30 minutes at most, then end it.

    Before the feed: play first, or feed straight away?

    Feed soon after he wakes from a nap, within about 15 minutes. Change his nappy, then give the bottle. Babies usually feed best when they're rested. If he's tired, he'll fuss and refuse. Spacing the feeds out is what makes him hungry, not tiring him out.

    Other things worth trying

    • Teat size: at 6 months he may need a faster flow, maybe the next size up. If the flow is too slow, he'll get frustrated and give up. Try one size up for a day and see if he takes more.
    • Solids: give these about an hour after a milk feed, not just before one. Otherwise they fill him up.
    • Movement: some distracted feeders take more while you walk around or rock them gently, or facing outward on your lap. It's worth an experiment.
    • Mornings: the first feed of the day is usually his biggest. Make sure it's a full bottle.
